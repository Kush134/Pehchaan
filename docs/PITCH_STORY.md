## Narration and on-screen takeaways

### 01. Pehchaan, in one sentence

Pehchaan helps Hindi- and Marathi-speaking kirana owners turn repeat-customer transaction patterns into approved, margin-aware actions. We start with one neighborhood shopkeeper and one recurring growth decision.

**On screen:** A growth copilot for a specific kirana owner / Turn repeat-customer patterns into approved, margin-aware actions.

### 02. Exactly who we are building for

Our initial customer is an owner-operated kirana in Wakad, Pune: one shop, one to three people, Hindi or Marathi speaking, accepting QR payments, and serving recurring neighborhood households. They have usable, consented transaction history but no growth analyst. This is our chosen launch segment.

**On screen:** One kirana · Wakad, Pune · 1–3-person team / Hindi/Marathi · QR payments · Recurring local households

### 03. A recurring decision, not a rare event

We design for daily sales checks and weekly retention reviews. A customer who normally visits every five days but has been absent for twenty deserves attention. How often owners miss these signals still needs field validation.

**On screen:** Daily sales checks. Weekly retention decisions. / Example: usual cadence 5 days; inactivity 20 days.

### 04. What is the cost of missing the signal?

Consider an illustrative month: ten regulars miss two purchases each, at three hundred rupees per basket. That puts six thousand rupees of revenue at risk. An assumed twenty-two percent gross margin means thirteen hundred and twenty rupees of gross profit at risk. Twenty minutes of checking across twenty-six days adds eight hours forty minutes. These are assumptions, not measured losses.

**On screen:** 10 regulars × 2 missed visits × ₹300 = ₹6,000 revenue at risk/month / Assumed 22% margin: ₹1,320 gross profit at risk · Time scenario: 8h 40m

### 05. Useful products already exist

Paytm's payment-gateway analytics already includes repeat customers, RFM and cohorts. Vyapar supports billing and inventory; loyalty platforms already offer personalized engagement. Our wedge is not inventing these capabilities. It is testing a voice-guided path from insight to a margin-checked, approved action for this owner.

**On screen:** Paytm: customer analytics · Vyapar: billing and inventory / Loyalty tools: rewards and segments · Our wedge: the approved-action workflow

### 06. Three core capabilities

The product has three core capabilities: a Comeback workflow, a local-language Voice Copilot, and a Haggle workflow with mandate guardrails. Catalogues, imports, analytics and audit records support these workflows, rather than becoming ten separate products.

**On screen:** 1. Comeback workflow   2. Voice Copilot   3. Haggle with guardrails / Three hero capabilities, supported by shared data and controls.

### 07. Demo one: recognize the returning customer

Open a pseudonymous customer to inspect recency, frequency, spending and visit cadence. Purchase history explains the signal. New-customer safeguards prevent premature lost-customer labels. The records describe this store, not purchases at competitors.

**On screen:** Purchase history explains the customer signal / Personal visit cadence + RFM + spend change, with safeguards for new customers.

### 08. Prepare an eligible next action

That signal becomes a reviewable campaign: fifteen rupees off a two-hundred-and-fifty-rupee minimum bill. Audience and budget remain editable. Python checks contribution-margin rules, and separate merchant approval launches the campaign.

**On screen:** ₹15 off a ₹250 minimum bill / Editable audience and budget. Margin checks first; merchant approval before launch.

### 09. Close the loop with evidence

A and B offers are compared with a control group. Simulated redemptions update the ledger. Compare conversion and discount expense; incremental-profit estimates stay preliminary until sufficient observations and statistical evidence support them.

**On screen:** Compare the offer against a control group / Synthetic outcomes are recorded; preliminary profit is not presented as proven uplift.

### 10. Demo two: approve it in a familiar language

The merchant speaks Hindi or Marathi. Genuine Sarvam recognition and interpretation prepare a structured proposal, followed by separate merchant approval. Saved conversations and text input make this same workflow easier to access and resume.

**On screen:** Speak → transcribe → interpret → review → approve / The language model proposes an action; the merchant authorizes it.

### 11. Demo three: negotiate within the mandate

For bulk orders, a Buyer Agent compares three Seller Agents through four LangGraph rounds. Python calculates eligible deals within budget, stock and delivery constraints. The final order still needs merchant approval and buyer confirmation.

**On screen:** Three seller agents. Four bounded rounds. / The selected bundle must satisfy budget, stock, delivery and approval constraints.

### 12. Trust is a rule, not a promise

A ninety-five-thousand-rupee proposal against a forty-thousand-rupee budget is blocked. Python enforces mandates independently of the model, with signatures, expiry and replay checks. This prototype uses synthetic records and simulated payments; no real Paytm money moves.

**On screen:** ₹95,000 against a ₹40,000 budget: BLOCKED / Deterministic rules and explicit approvals keep authority outside the model.

### 13. Our difference is the connected workflow

Our difference is the connected workflow: signal, eligible proposal, local-language review, approval and feedback. The prototype implements that loop. Whether this owner finds it easier and more valuable than existing tools remains a pilot question.

**On screen:** Signal → proposal → local-language review → approval → feedback / A differentiated workflow hypothesis, not a claim that competitors are failing.

### 14. A company built around merchant value

Our proposed model combines basic insights with a paid merchant-growth subscription for advanced workflows. We will test willingness to pay against owner time saved and contribution profit. Pricing, subscription billing and a commercial Paytm partnership are not yet validated or implemented.

**On screen:** Proposed: basic insights + a paid merchant-growth subscription / Validate willingness to pay against time saved and contribution profit.

### 15. The next step is a focused pilot

We propose ten eligible kirana shops over four weeks, measuring workflow time, weekly return visits, discount expense and contribution profit using consented data and appropriate controls. This is a validation plan, not existing traction.

**On screen:** Proposed pilot: 10 kiranas × 4 weeks / Measure return visits, discount expense, contribution profit and owner time.

### 16. The merchant's next payment

One kirana owner misses a useful customer signal. Pehchaan turns it into an approved, margin-aware action and visible feedback. A specific customer, a focused product, and a measurable next step: the neighborhood kirana's growth copilot.

**On screen:** Pehchaan helps the neighborhood kirana earn the next payment / A specific customer, a focused product, and a measurable validation plan.
